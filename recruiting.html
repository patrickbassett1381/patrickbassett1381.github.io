3 · Recruiting prep · Units U-301 to U-305

From fit to offer.

MBA recruiting is a loop: decide which roles fit, talk to the people in them, tailor a resume to each posting, then interview. I built a unit for each stage. They share one story bank of my experiences and one tracker of where each application stands, so a fact I confirm once is used everywhere, and nothing is ever invented.

U-301 · LIVE

RunsWeekdays 6:45 AM

CalendlyGoogle CalendarGmailWeb researchClaude scheduled task

Coffee-chat prep

A booking page where people pick Zoom or in person, plus a morning job that writes a one-page brief for each chat that day and puts it on my calendar five minutes before we talk.

Problem

Recruiting means many short conversations. I was relying on memory to keep track of who said what and what I meant to ask, and prep often got skipped for people I hadn't met yet.

Solution

The brief starts from my own notes on the person: what they shared when booking, past emails and past conversations. Then it adds public background and company news.

Result

~30 contacts each have a one-page brief: our past conversations, my open follow-ups and what I want to ask. Every chat starts where the last one left off.

Sample · fictional personPrep brief

Prep: Dana Reyes · Director of Strategy, Northfold Storage

Wed 11:00 AM · Zoom · booked 2 days ago · topic: "moving from consulting into energy storage"

Top three
  • Her move from consulting into a storage developer mirrors the path you're exploring.
  • Northfold announced a new manufacturing site last month: ask what it changed for strategy.
  • You met her briefly at the spring energy panel; she mentioned hiring MBA interns.
30-second intro

ChemE, eight years in solar and chemicals strategy, now at Haas focused on energy and climate...

Closing ask

Is there one person on her team I should talk to next?

Design decisionOne run a day instead of hourly. A 12-hour booking notice and a 6 PM cutoff mean the day's chats are fixed by early morning, so a single run covers everything. It never edits the guest's invite and never emails anyone.

U-302 · LIVE

RunsWhen I update a tracker

Claude skillExcelGoogle Drive

Industry and function fit

A function matrix maps the functions I'm interested in against my target industries: where my fit is strongest today and which gaps to fill, so I know where to focus. Alongside it sit a recruiting tracker of companies and application status and a networking list. The AI reads read-only snapshots of all three to ground every resume, brief and interview prep.

Design decisionEach spreadsheet has one master in Drive. Changes always flow master → snapshot, never the other way, and every version notes what changed and why inside the file, so there is only ever one live version to trust.

U-303 · LIVE

RunsWhen I paste a job posting

Claude skillNode.jsPython validatorGoogle Drive

Resume tailoring

Paste a job posting and it reads the role, picks the strongest bullets from my story bank, and shows me the set it chose, what it cut and the close calls. It stops for my critique, then builds the resume in the school's format from code and runs a validator on it.

  • One folder per application, holding the resume, the posting text (postings disappear), the final bullets and the numbers I must verify before submitting.
  • One sentence to finish: "finalize <company>" runs the closing steps: final PDFs, approved bullets back into the pool, and the tracker status updated.
  • A story bank marks each story READY or GAP. A gap can't supply a number until I fill it in an interview-style session.

Honesty floorIt never invents, rounds up or "improves" a number. New facts are confirmed with me before they're saved, and confidential details from past jobs stay vague on anything public.

U-304 · LIVE

RunsOn demand

Claude skillWeb researchbCourses alumni search

Interview drills

A practice partner for behavioral interviews, scored against the six-beat storytelling structure taught by Haas career coaches: title, setting, challenge, actions, results, impact. Plus delivery checks: about two minutes, specific moments and numbers, and the balance of "I" and "we."

  • Story drill: a question in the interviewer's words, a scorecard, the single biggest fix, and two hard follow-ups.
  • Mock round: five or six questions from a persona for the track, no feedback until the end, and never the same story twice in one loop.
  • Pitches: "Tell me about yourself" and "why this company," one version per track.
  • Company prep: what the team needs now, likely questions, which story answers each one, and Haas alumni who work there.
  • Recorded-mock review: scores my answers from a recorded practice session, then discards the transcript.

Design decisionIt coaches the beats, not a script. It returns an outline in my own words, so answers stay natural, and it never softens the critique of a weak answer.

What this area will never do

Never contacts anyone

No emails, invites or messages go out on my behalf. Drafts wait for me.

Never invents a fact

Every claim on a resume or in a pitch traces to a story I confirmed.

Keeps confidences

Sensitive details from past employers stay general, in writing and in practice answers.

Stores the minimum

Practice transcripts and drill scores are never saved.
