Class prep sheets

I do the readings and cases myself. The prep sheet is my one-page brief for the room: it organizes that prep so the points I want to make are ready when the discussion starts. Each opens with the lesson frame (what the session is trying to teach, how each reading fits, the questions to be ready for), then takes the format the session needs.

  • Reading brief: the key points and my takeaways, about a page per source.
  • Case memo: case setup, a facts table recomputed from the exhibits, the decision through each lens (finance, people, CEO, outsider), biases at play, and my recommendation.
  • Discussion prep: a shorter version for discussion-only cases, built around the points I want to raise.
  • Quant prep: a table of problem types with their traps, a method recipe, a worked example and the formulas.

Concept Log digest

Turns everything I captured into one running Concept Log per course: my handwritten slide annotations, typed class notes, discussion-section captions, and what the professor flagged as testable in the lecture recordings.

Problem

My notes were scattered across typed files, a handwriting tablet and class recordings. Nothing connected them at exam time.

Solution

Read my ink on the slides and only the moments the professor flagged as exam material, then write each concept as rule, method, trap and a tiny worked example.

Result

One source of truth per course. The AI reads only the moments professors flag as testable, a small slice of each transcript, which keeps the recording step cheap.

Sample · fictional entryConcept Log

Contribution margin per bottleneck hour Shaky

Operations · L7 · Last practiced Oct 14 · ★ flagged in lecture

Rule

When capacity is the limit, rank products by margin per hour of the bottleneck, not margin per unit.

Trap

Problem set 3, Q2: I ranked by unit margin and picked the wrong product mix.

Tiny example

A earns $40 in 2 kiln-hours ($20/h); B earns $30 in 1 ($30/h). Make B first.

Design decisionA cheap script gathers and filters; the AI only judges. The sync script builds a nightly queue of notes no log has seen, and every digested source gets a ledger line, so a class is never digested twice and the run only opens logs with something new.

Homework coach

Works through problem sets with me: I attempt each problem, it checks my work and gives the smallest useful hint, and it switches to full explanations when I ask. At the end, what I struggled with is logged to the Concept Log as "Shaky," followed by a short quiz.

Integrity firstBefore reading any assignment, it checks that course's AI policy. Assignments that ban AI are listed only, never opened. On graded work it explains the concepts in full, but the answers stay mine.

Exam kit

Three tools that turn the Concept Log into exam material.

  • Past-exam analysis: tags every past question by type and weights topics by their share of points.
  • Printable cheat sheet, built from the Concept Log with my "Shaky" items first.
  • Review deck: a code-built PowerPoint of the professor's key slides, each with "Why it matters," "Trap" and "Exam angle." Topics get slides in proportion to their exam weight.

Design decisionIf I haven't taken the practice exam yet, the deck leaves it out entirely, with no questions or hints that give answers away. After I take it timed, the traps are rebuilt around what I actually missed.

Weekly class review

Every Sunday: every real deadline for the next four weeks, with stepping stones planned backward from it and clearly marked DUE or Step.

  • Collision check: two major items in any three-day window, and flexible work moves earlier.
  • Group work: proposes two meeting times from my free calendar slots, and drafts a paste-ready update for the team I project-manage.
  • Look-back: what each course covered, and a three-question quiz on concepts I haven't practiced in a week.
